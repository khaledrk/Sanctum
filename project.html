PROJECT / 01

RUINS
REGROUNDED

SHORNEMEAD FORT / GRAVESEND / THAMES ESTUARY

Ruins Regrounded began with a walk from Gravesend through its industrial edge and marshes to Shornemead Fort, where decay coexists with graffiti, informal occupation and ecological life. Repeated visits and a field-recording device mapped ruination as Decay, Subversion and Assimilation. These conditions generated three design responses: Continuation, Acceleration and Cohabitation. Projected toward 2100 and rising tides, selected structures are removed, opened or retained while new circulation and habitat are introduced. The fort becomes a changing social and ecological gathering ground for people, water, vegetation and other forms of life.

READ + RECORD / 01

History, traces and field instruments

Ruination is mapped as a sequence of military use, abandonment, informal occupation and ecological return. The recorder turns observation into a temporary act of inhabitation.

POSITION / 02

Work with what is already acting

Continuation retains active traces. Acceleration directs selected processes of change. Cohabitation negotiates human and non-human occupation.

INHABIT / 03

A route through fort, marsh and tide

Platforms touch the ruin selectively. Existing fabric, open water and colonising vegetation remain legible as separate but connected systems.

FUTURE OCCUPATION / 04

Let use exceed authorship

The final views test ordinary, improvised and seasonal occupations rather than a single prescribed programme.
