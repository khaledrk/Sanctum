PROJECT / 01

RUINS
REGROUNDED

Shornemead Fort / Gravesend / Thames Estuary

ABSTRACT — MAX 100 WORDS

Ruins Regrounded begins with a former military fort encountered through the industrial edge and marshes of Gravesend. Fieldwork mapped ruination as three active conditions: decay, subversion and assimilation. Each was tested through continuation, acceleration and cohabitation. Projecting toward 2100, the proposal does not defend the fort from higher tides. Water is allowed in, unsafe fragments may be selectively removed and reused, and new routes support future dwelling, gathering and ecological succession. The fort remains social, unfinished and occupied by human and non-human life.

01 / FIELD

A journey through active ruination

The route from Gravesend to Shornemead moves from damaged industrial edges into an engineered but apparently unattended marsh. Broken streets, damaged fences, rubbish, grazing horses, graffiti, wet ground and military fragments made ruination visible as an ongoing condition rather than an ending.

02 / RECORD

Frame what is already happening

A field instrument was used to document ground conditions while walking the site. Mapping, photography, sound recording, observation and historical research were brought together to identify recurring forms of ruination.

03 / INTERPRET

Decay. Subversion. Assimilation.

Decay describes material failure. Subversion describes alternative use by human or non-human actors. Assimilation describes conditions being overtaken by water, vegetation, sediment and other forces.

01

Decay

Fracture, collapse, corrosion, abrasion and the slow failure of designed material.

02

Subversion

Tracks, occupation, graffiti, grazing, shelter and uses that exceed original programme.

03

Assimilation

Seepage, pooling, mud, vegetation, sediment and ecological succession taking hold.

04 / RESPOND

Do not begin with intervention.

Each condition is tested through three possible responses: continue a process already underway, accelerate it where change is productive, or cohabit with it by adding only what supports existing and future use.

05 / 2100

Allow the tide in.

The future proposal assumes higher water and changing forms of dwelling around the marsh. Rather than walling the fort off, it accepts hydrological change, increases human and non-human encounter, selectively removes unsafe fabric and reuses debris as part of a more social and ecological ground.
