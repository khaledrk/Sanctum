03 / PARTNERS + CLIENTS

The work needs other knowledge.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS proposes a practice built with those who live, maintain, study, remember, regulate and occupy changing landscapes. The organisations below form a prospective network—not a claim of existing partnership—selected for the Shornemead project and for future work across damaged estuarine ground.

COMMISSIONERS + STEWARDS

Land ownership, heritage consent, flood adaptation, planning, funding and long-term care.

COMMUNITIES + SPACE USERS

Memory, daily routes, informal repair, conflict, access and knowledge carried through use.

ECOLOGISTS + RESEARCHERS

Hydrology, succession, contamination, species, archives, oral histories and long-term monitoring.

MAKERS + CULTURAL PARTNERS

Fabrication, material tests, surveying, sound, film, public programmes and environmental storytelling.

SHORNEMEAD / WORKING CONSTELLATION

Start with the actors already responsible for the ground.

Shornemead Fort is a Scheduled Monument inside a protected estuarine landscape and has been owned by the RSPB since 2000. Any credible commission therefore begins with ecology, heritage, flood defence and public use in the same room.

01

LANDOWNER + STEWARD

RSPB

Lead site partner for habitat management, grazing marsh, bird life, access and the fort’s long-term stewardship.

02

HERITAGE AUTHORITY

Historic England

Scheduled Monument advice, archaeological evidence and consent for work affecting surviving and buried fabric.

03

FLOOD + CLIMATE CLIENT

Environment Agency / TE2100

Adaptive tidal flood planning, monitoring and the integration of public access, habitat and future defences.

04

LOCAL AUTHORITY

Gravesham Borough Council

Local planning, biodiversity strategy, green-blue infrastructure and connections between Gravesend, Shorne and the river.

05

RIVER AUTHORITY + FUNDER

Port of London Authority

Navigation, foreshore and river ecology expertise, with a funding route for community-led environmental work.

06

CROSS-SECTOR RESEARCH

Thames Estuary Partnership

A forum for data, green-blue infrastructure, heritage, recreation and negotiation between estuary stakeholders.

07

ECOLOGICAL ADVISER

Natural England

Advice on protected habitats, species, recreation pressure and nature recovery across the Thames Estuary and Marshes.

08

HABITAT + SPECIES

Kent Wildlife Trust

County-scale restoration knowledge, wetland management, species monitoring and volunteer stewardship.

09

CITIZEN SCIENCE + ACCESS

Thames21

Community-led river care, practical participation, monitoring and long-term public ownership of waterway projects.

10

ESTUARINE HERITAGE

Medway Swale Estuary Partnership

A nearby model for joining natural and cultural heritage, research, local knowledge and sustainable estuary management.

11

ART + PUBLIC ENQUIRY

Cement Fields

North Kent cultural commissioning grounded in shifting industrial landscapes, slow research and non-extractive community relationships.

12

RESEARCH + MAKING

University of Greenwich

Landscape research, fabrication, prototyping and the academic setting in which the Ruins Recorder and project were developed.

13

LOCAL KNOWLEDGE

Walkers + birders

Repeated observation of access, seasonal change, species, paths and the social life of the reserve.

14

INFORMAL OCCUPATION

Riders + fishers + explorers

Evidence of use beyond official programmes: the practices that reveal conflict, risk, attachment and possibility.

HOW WE WORK / 01—05

  1. Enter through the existing steward.
  2. Record before proposing.
  3. Put lived and non-human evidence beside statutory evidence.
  4. Prototype the smallest useful intervention.
  5. Return, monitor and revise.
