ABOUT / PRACTICE

EREÍPIAPÓLIS

EREÍPIAPÓLIS investigates ruinous landscapes and the lives that continue through them.

The practice works through repeated visits, historical research, mapping, listening, photography, conversation, modelling and design.

It focuses on careful interventions that can document, reveal, support or redirect the processes already acting on a place.

NAME

Two cities shaped the name.

I am from Asyut in Upper Egypt. Discovering the city through its older Greek histories and the name Lycopolis opened another way of looking at a place I thought I already knew.

I grew up in Jeddah. When I returned after the neighbourhood and many of the places I remembered had been demolished, I encountered the city through absence, memory and ruins.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS carries both experiences into a practice concerned with forgotten, abandoned and destroyed places, and with the afterlives that continue through them.

MANIFESTO
  1. Nothing ends where it breaks.

    Destruction changes a place. It does not remove its capacity for another life.

  2. Go there more than once.

    Weather, people, animals, tides and materials reveal different things each time.

  3. Read the damage.

    Cracks, corrosion, rubble, erosion and collapse are evidence.

  4. Map what the map misses.

    Routes, sounds, memories, habits, absences and informal occupation belong in the drawing.

  5. Follow use before intention.

    What people and other species do with a place can matter more than what it was designed to do.

  6. Leave something alone.

    Restraint is a design decision.

  7. Work with what is already acting.

    Water, sediment, vegetation, weathering and occupation are part of the project.

  8. Decay can be directed.

    Retain, remove, accelerate, repair, open or add according to what the site needs.

  9. Every addition will become a ruin.

    Design for change, weathering and uses you cannot predict.

  10. Draw time.

    History, present occupation and future conditions belong in the same field.

  11. Keep the future unfinished.

    A project should leave room for others to alter it.

  12. Humans are one inhabitant among many.

    Plants, animals, water and other forces participate in the landscape.

  13. Use making to think.

    Models, fabrication, drawing and prototypes test positions before they become fixed.

  14. Pass the knowledge on.

    Recording, mapping and showing should make the site legible to others.

  15. Return. Revise. Return again.

    The place keeps changing, so the reading must change with it.
