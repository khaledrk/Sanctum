01 / ABOUT

Places have afterlives.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS reads damaged, abandoned and changing landscapes through fragments, traces and agencies, then works with the futures already emerging from them.

NAME / ORIGIN

EREÍPIAPÓLIS

I come from Asyut in Upper Egypt. Its older histories, including Lycopolis, opened another way of seeing a city I thought I already knew.

I grew up in Jeddah. My neighbourhood was destroyed, along with many of the places I loved. Returning meant encountering the city through absence, memory and ruins.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS carries those experiences into a practice concerned with forgotten, abandoned and destroyed places, and with the lives and landscapes that continue after them.

POSITION / 05 SENTENCES

We begin with repeated visits, lived knowledge and what the official map leaves out.

We read decay, informal occupation and ecological succession as active evidence.

We work through continuation, acceleration and cohabitation instead of default restoration.

We use drawing, recording and making to test the smallest consequential intervention.

We return to see what changed, who arrived and what the work must become next.
