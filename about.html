01 / ABOUT

PLACES HAVE AFTERLIVES.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS works through repeated visits, research, mapping, sound recording, observation and selective intervention. The practice follows damaged spaces across time: what survives, what returns, what gets used again and what remains only in memory.

THE NAME

ASYUT / NICOPOLIS
JEDDAH / AFTERLIFE

I am from Asyut in Upper Egypt. Historically, it was known as Lycopolis: another name that revealed another dimension of the city. Jeddah is my adopted hometown, the city where I grew up. My neighbourhood was destroyed. Every part of the city I loved was destroyed. When I returned, it felt like a city of ruins.

EREÍPIAPÓLIS comes from that experience. The name gives another dimension to places that have been forgotten, abandoned or destroyed: places that survive in memory, and in the lives that continue after them.

METHOD / RETURN

Experience the space.
Follow the questions it creates.
Return with more questions.

Map what the map misses. Record sound, movement, weathering and use. Build models to test what a drawing cannot answer. Position an intervention. Return to see what changed.

LEARNING / MAKING / SHOWING

Research moves through the hand.

Learning gathers histories, field evidence and other forms of expertise. Making uses 3D modelling, physical models, fabrication, material tests and prototypes as ways of thinking. Showing makes the work public through drawings, installations, workshops, conversations and publications.
