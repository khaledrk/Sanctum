ABOUT / PRACTICE POSITION

AFTER THE
INTENDED LIFE

IRIBIOPOLIS investigates what can happen in places that appear done, over or abandoned, and asks what forms of life, use and knowledge continue after their intended function begins to fail.

WHAT THE NAME SUGGESTS

IRIBIOPOLIS names a practice concerned with places in transition rather than finished objects.

It describes a field where material, ecological and social lives overlap after intended functions loosen.

The name therefore points toward a way of practising across ruins, marshes, infrastructures and other unstable grounds.

FIVE POSITIONS
01

Investigate before intervening.

Multiple visits, walking, historical research, mapping, sound recording, photography, discussion and observation are used to understand what a place is already doing.

02

A ruin is not an ending.

Material failure can coincide with new social, cultural and ecological life. The practice looks for those afterlives rather than treating damage as an empty condition.

03

Design can be limited.

The appropriate response may be to continue an existing process, accelerate a productive change, cohabit with it, or decide that no new object is required.

04

Knowledge remains in the ground.

Tracks, debris, water, vegetation, informal occupation, memories and maintenance practices are treated as evidence rather than noise to be cleared away.

05

Ruination will urbanise too.

As cities, infrastructures and climates change, the practice treats designing with increasing ruined and unstable sites as a landscape discipline in its own right.
